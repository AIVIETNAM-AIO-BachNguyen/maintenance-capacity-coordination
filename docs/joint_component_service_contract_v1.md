# Joint component service contract — JCST/1, revision 1.1

Status 2026-10-25: implemented for engineering validation only. This is a distinct physical and information contract. Historical machine-level replay, v1–v22 verdicts, and TEST remain unchanged; absolute costs across contracts are incomparable. The [primary-source audit](rodriguez_2022_primary_and_gap_audit.md) separates verified facts from assumptions. Rodríguez (2022) already permits technician–component choice or delay. The action tuple itself is not a novelty claim.

## State, public information, and decisions

There are M series machines, each with C_m ≥ 1 components, and J technicians. At boundary t, component (m,c) has nonnegative integer operating age a[m,c] and failure flag f[m,c]. Each technician has either no job or a job (m,c,j,kind,remaining), where kind is PM or CM and remaining is a positive integer. At most one job targets a machine and at most one job occupies a technician. A machine's derived mode is service if assigned, otherwise breakdown if any component failed, otherwise working. No request state or queue restricts starts.

The centralized observation contains t, every current component age/failure flag, every technician's target/kind/remaining, and the entire public configuration: Weibull scales/shapes, duration/compatibility tensor, costs, horizon and tail rule. All engineering controllers use this observation and the full legal catalogue. Neither sampled future failure times nor actual future uniforms are observed. A model-based controller may use the public transition law and fresh forecast seeds; its model access and compute are reported.

A complete action is a set of triples (m,c,j) with idle machine m, free technician j, compatible positive duration d[m,c,j], and no repeated machine or technician. The empty set is WAIT. Every idle machine can choose a healthy component for PM at any age, including below legacy thresholds and while another component is failed. A failed target gets CM. A machine or technician already in service cannot start another job. Action validation precedes transition. Starts in the same action are simultaneous and tuple order has no physical meaning. There is no collision fine, forced restart or requester fee.

## Hazard, event order, and accounting

For scale α[m,c] > 0, shape β[m,c] > 0, and age a, the conditional interval failure probability is:

q[m,c](a) = 1 − exp(−[((a+1)/α[m,c])^β[m,c] − (a/α[m,c])^β[m,c]]).

Equivalently q(a) = 1 − S(a+1)/S(a), where Weibull survival S(x) = exp(−(x/α)^β). Independent U(seed,t,m,c) are generated by a stable keyed hash, independent of action enumeration. A healthy component fails iff U < q(a) when its machine operates for the full interval. Forecast seeds are separate from physical seeds by role. This conditional operating-age law, independent hazards and age freeze during downtime are JCST modeling assumptions, not attributed to Rodríguez.

At each boundary, in this exact order:

1. Validate state and complete action. Record PM/CM from each target's pre-start failure flag, then create jobs with remaining = d[m,c,j] simultaneously.
2. For [t,t+1), a machine produces iff it has no failed component and no active job after starts. Charge unavailability once per machine for this interval. Charge PM/CM once at start. A failed or serviced machine does not operate; all its component ages and failure flags freeze until targeted completion.
3. On every producing machine, evaluate each healthy component's hazard at its saved age a, then increment its operating age by one. At the end of the interval, apply keyed uniforms. Charge failure cost per new failed component. End failures affect production starting at [t+1,t+2); they do not add downtime to [t,t+1).
4. Decrement every active job, including jobs started at t, by one. On reaching zero at boundary t+1, reset only its target to age zero and healthy, log completion, and release worker and machine. A duration-one job occupies [t,t+1) and is next assignable at t+1. Other failed flags persist. Failure and completion can occur at the same boundary on different machines.
5. Observe the resulting state at t+1 before the next action.

For interval t, C_t = c_PM N_PM + c_CM N_CM + c_U N_unavailable + c_F N_new_failures, with finite nonnegative simulation-unit coefficients. Reward is −C_t. On the final controlled step, the adapter also includes the fixed tail cost in its returned reward. There are no other charges. The event ledger records starts, failures, completions and each occupied job interval. H controlled ticks are 0..H−1; the fixed public risk/duration controller owns ticks H..H+B−1. Total evaluation cost is the separate H sum plus B tail sum. There is no terminal salvage or extra tick charge. Unfinished jobs and failures at H+B are reported without further charge. B=0 is valid.

## Engineering fixture, not a calibrated benchmark

The small default fixture has three machines, two components per machine, two technicians, all compatible, Weibull (α,β) of (6,5) and (8,5) by component, and reciprocal durations [[2,8],[8,2]] by component and technician on every machine. Costs are c_PM=1, c_CM=2, c_U=6, c_F=15; H=48, B=16. Initial state for the reproducible fixture has every component healthy at age zero and every technician free. The code also accepts explicit validated initial states. Future initial-age distributions must be specified before scientific collection. These hazard/duration values echo Rodríguez's small examples, while costs reuse old simulation-unit labels; neither source calibrates this benchmark. Engineering smoke seeds and settings are never selected by performance rank.

For idle, all-compatible 3 × 2 × 2, the full action catalogue has 1 + 12 + 24 = 37 actions. The count is specific to that state/configuration. Full root enumeration is feasible there; it is not exact optimization over a stochastic horizon.

## Assumptions and research boundary

The series production rule, one service per machine, one job per technician, non-preemption, component operating-age freeze, perfect target-only restoration, deterministic duration and compatibility, opportunistic PM during breakdown, independent conditional Weibull hazards, current full information and fixed tail are JCST/1 assumptions. Travel, spares, shifts, imperfect restoration, joint degradation and production scheduling are absent. A changed physical or information rule needs a new version and protocol.

Implementation and bounded smoke establish semantics, not economic benefit. A future study must distinguish (i) lower economic cost at matched information, action and suitable compute budgets from (ii) acceptable cost with lower compute, inference or sample budget. It must separately test reachability, economic consequence, present-information selection, headroom beyond strong controls and amortization. Scientific configuration and practical gates must be declared before outcomes; TEST remains closed.
