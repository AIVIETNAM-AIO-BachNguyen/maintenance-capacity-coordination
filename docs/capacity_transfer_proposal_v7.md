# Service-duration transfer as a demand–capacity robustness test

Date: 2026-10-22 (Asia/Taipei). This is a new deployment-robustness question using the existing N4/K2 simulator and frozen v3 PPO policies. It does not amend the failed v3 or v6 gates, reopen their TEST sets, or introduce a new scorer, reward, request contract, failure law or physical action. See `capacity_transfer_check_v7.md` for the locked diagnostic.

## Operational question

Maintenance timing and technician commitment depend on service duration. A policy trained when a compatible technician takes two or six ticks may face one or three ticks at another site, while a specialist/generalist system may move from two/eight to three/twelve ticks. The existing `family_configs("full")` contains these N4/K2 configurations; all retain the same failure law, costs, restoration, threshold requests, automatic CM, complete matching/WAIT and non-preemptive service. The actor inputs already include each machine–technician service duration, restoration, current free/busy state and remaining commitment. This is variation of a known model parameter, not a new simulator semantic.

V3's structured S policy shares machine and worker encoders and scores legal matchings through machine–technician edge features. Its flat G3 PPO control receives the identical present inputs and full action mask, with near-matched actor size and the same training budget. The numerical remaining-time ablation R tests whether that subset of commitment context matters. V3's original-source held-out S−G3 was directionally favorable but missed its locked practical threshold, and S−R was near zero. V4/v6 did not establish a structural benefit. None of those panels tested transfer to changed service durations. The transfer question is therefore distinct from repeating v3 on favorable seeds or revising v6's failed architecture.

## Falsifiable claim and decision

Test the already-selected, audited v3 S/R/G3 checkpoints without retraining or selection on two **preselected** target configurations: reciprocal scale 2 → reciprocal scale 1, and specialist/generalist scale 2 → scale 3. Also reevaluate the source configurations on the same fresh physical seed IDs to measure change in the S−G3 gap. The primary estimand is paired target H+tail cost S−G3; a difference-in-differences compares that gap with the source panel. The R contrast is required before attributing any gain to numerical technician commitment context. Full-information G3 is the same-information/action/budget attribution control; D3, historical fixed-threshold G and always-request D, frozen V14, risk matching and finite six-tick F2 are same-panel contextual/strong references with disclosed budgets/contracts.

A predeclared gate must show a practical target S−G3 gain, less degradation than G3, and a numerical-context S−R gain. If it fails, retain the negative robustness result and stop v7. If it passes, independently retrain the unchanged v3 arms on fresh source seeds with the same PPO/DEV rule and evaluate the same transfer question on fresh physical seeds before proposing any TEST. This check cannot establish variable N/K transfer, a calibrated machine value or two independent policies. It can support only the named N4/K2 service-duration robustness claim against the named controls.
