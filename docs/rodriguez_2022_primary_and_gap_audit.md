| Machines/components | §3.1, pp.5–6, Fig.3, Table 4: identical parallel machines, each with component set `C_m`; component failures determine working, breakdown or maintenance machine mode. **Verified source fact:** Rodríguez already joins timing, component choice and skilled technician choice. Merely adding those three to this repository is **not** a literature novelty claim. **Proposal:** use a versioned component-level contract to make the intended decision implementable here, then test whether a component-demand/technician-commitment representation improves *economic outcomes or efficiency over strong same-information controls*. The simulator upgrade, stronger evaluation and possible method gain are three different contributions. A favorable method effect is an **empirical question**.

### Other primary evidence and limits

| Primary paper | Supported mechanism | What it does not license here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Wang & Gombolay, ICAPS 2022](https://cdn.aaai.org/ojs/19839/19839-40-23852-1-2-20220613.pdf), pp.529–530 | Dynamic, stochastic predictive maintenance scheduling and a graph policy for resource allocation are established prior art. Its AirME setting has aircraft subsystem tasks and busy crews. | It explicitly uses homogeneous crews in its graph simplification (p.530), so it does not establish our heterogeneous technician effect or graph novelty. |
| [Demirci, Arts & van Houtum, 2024/2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10696-024-09544-y), abstract | Condition-based decisions across deteriorating machines with limited simultaneous maintenance workers and cost/revenue tradeoffs are established prior art. | Its specific degradation economics and repairman assumptions do not validate our Weibull, duration or cost numbers. |
| [Joint maintenance operation selection and technician routing, 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cor.2024.106667), abstract | Selection of operations, skill/availability matching and routing have been studied together. | Routing and geographic travel are outside the proposed minimal contract; do not claim joint selection alone as new. |
| [Khatab et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cie.2018.09.012), abstract | Multi-component selective maintenance and repairperson assignment have been optimized jointly. | Its mission/break and imperfect-maintenance setting is different from continuous tick-by-tick decisions; it does not calibrate our plant. |

These are sources for **operational mechanisms and prior-work boundaries**, not evidence that any proposed method will win. Real maintenance logs would help external calibration of component hazards, skill durations and downtime costs; they are **not** a prerequisite for a simulator-internal falsifiable study.

**Verified current-code fact:** the old simulator is a machine-level abstraction with threshold-admitted service-start matching. **Proposal:** preserve it as DC-F0/1 historical baseline and replay target; do not call its H+tail costs numerically comparable to a new component simulator. **Empirical question:** whether the new contract generates reachable, material, present-information-selectable component timing/assignment choices that remain valuable against strong controls.
