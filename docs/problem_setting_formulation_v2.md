## Decision and claim boundary

**Choose the existing threshold-admitted service-start/matching contract (DC-F0/1) as the first testable setting.** A machine's *maintenance decision* within this scope is whether its admitted PM starts now or it continues operating while waiting; the shared scheduler chooses the worker and can WAIT. The capacity side supplies compatibility, service duration, restoration and existing non-preemptive assignments. This is a substantive demand–capacity decision, but it excludes PM before threshold, choice of work-order submission, cancellation, intervention type, routing and promised service deadline. The claim retained is *coordinated timing and assignment after admission*; the excluded claim is *optimization of the entire maintenance-request lifecycle*. This boundary follows the actual authority in source, not the performance of a graph actor. A policy may be centralized and use standard PPO, search or exact enumeration. No two-policy or algorithmic novelty is assumed.

This is a **conditional research choice**, not a declaration that F0 gives a useful learned gain. It keeps simulator semantics while an independent setting qualification measures reachability, economic consequence and present-information predictability. If F0 fails, report that scoped finding and revisit operational authority with independent justification; do not repair a failed gate by tuning a learner or selecting a favorable configuration. A direct joint timing contract remains a serious alternative, described below, but the v2 early-start screen missed its own practical gate; no evidence currently warrants activating it over F0.

### Demand, capacity and decisions

| Concept | Operational meaning | Status in DC-F0/1 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Condition/risk | Machine age `a_m`, failed flag `f_m`, known failure threshold `L` and probability `p`; at the next interval, a healthy unserved machine that reaches `L` has failure probability `p`. | Present age/failed/config are observed; future Bernoulli realization is hidden. Risk is derived/estimated, not an action. |
| Need for intervention | A request may be clinically/operationally reasonable before a threshold; the simulator does **not** encode a distinct latent need state. | No ground-truth demand label. A threshold PM order or failed-machine CM order is a rule-created eligibility record, not a calibrated need or value. |
| Economic value of service | Conditional expected cost difference between legal service-start/WAIT choices, including downstream occupancy and other machines. It can depend on the whole matching. | Latent decision value, estimated from the known model or learned; no per-machine scalar is automatically additive or transferable across capacity states. An embedding alone is not this value. |
| Work-order admission | Persistent PM/CM request in a queue; no capacity reservation and no charge until service starts. | Threshold submits PM; failure creates/upgrades CM. Policy cannot submit/cancel in F0. |
| Service start/assignment | Start on an admitted machine with a compatible free worker, or leave it productive (PM)/failed (CM) while waiting. | **Physical policy action** is a complete partial matching, including empty WAIT. Once started, the worker is occupied for `d_mj` intervals. |
| Capacity | Free/busy workers, `remaining_j`, assignment, compatibility `d_mj>0`, duration and restoration `r_mj`. | Present information. A start today removes an edge and worker now and changes future capacity; a busy worker's `remaining` is a prior commitment. |

The marginal value of starting machine `m` with worker `j` is context dependent: `Q(s, A)−Q(s, A∪{(m,j)})` depends on other chosen pairs and future states. Immediate coupling occurs through the one-machine/one-worker matching constraints. Multi-period coupling occurs because duration reserves `j`; timing coupling occurs because PM WAIT avoids today's downtime yet leaves the machine exposed to failure. If workers are abundant and interchangeable, durations/restoration identical, no worker bottleneck and costs/failure independent across machines, the decision can be nearly separable. Such cases are useful negative controls, not evidence that coordination is generally unnecessary.

## Exact operational contract from source

Evidence scope: the state/action/transition audit below was checked against current source. V2 and V13–V22 numerical verdicts are taken from their preserved findings/evidence map; this formulation review did not independently replay their raw artifacts and does not revise their audits.

Version label **DC-F0/1** names the existing composition of `pending_matching_completion_v1`, `shared_maintenance_requests_v1`, threshold `request_action` and H48+tail16 controller adapter. It does not alter code or version identifiers. Source: `src/ht_pdm_fjsp/maintenance_dispatch.py`, `maintenance_requests.py`, `maintenance_request_policies.py`, `maintenance_framework_v1.py`, `src/maintenance_capacity_coordination/{coordination_v1,scaled_coordination_v11}.py`. The current fixed N5/K3 configurations are `n5k3_residual_a/b` from `experiments/scaled_n5k3_v12_config.py` (configuration seeds 74740000/74740001). Their measured results are not used to select a new case.

Let `M={0,…,N−1}` be machines and `K={0,…,J−1}` workers. There is one maintenance operation per machine at a time: preventive (PM) while healthy, corrective (CM) after failure. Configuration `c=(L,p,a_max,d,r,c_PM,c_CM,c_U,c_F)`, where integer `d_mj=0` means incompatible, positive `d_mj` is deterministic service duration, and `0<r_mj≤1` is restoration fraction. The default cost coefficients are `1,2,6,15` simulation cost units respectively; they are engineering parameters, not calibrated monetary estimates. In each case `N=5,J=3`; the physical law does not depend on that size.

At boundary `t`, state is `s_t=(a_m,f_m,w_m; b_j,z_j; R_m,g_m)`: age, failed flag, physical failed waiting ticks, worker remaining time and assigned machine (`z_j=−1` if free), request record or none, and request generation. Request record includes PM/CM kind, creation time, optional upgrade/start times and worker. Time and `c` are part of controller information. `R_m` is not a reservation when queued. Initial ages are randomly drawn below `L`, all machines healthy and workers free under ordinary reset; explicit valid initial states are also supported by the lower-level environment.

Writing `Q_t={m:R_m is queued after rule admission}` and `F_t={j:b_j=0}`, the physical feasible set is

`𝒜(s_t)={A⊆Q_t×F_t : d_mj>0 for every (m,j)∈A, and no machine or worker appears twice}`.

The empty matching belongs to `𝒜(s_t)`. For an admissible present-information policy `π`, the finite-horizon objective is `min_π E_π[Σ_{t=0}^{47} C_t + Σ_{t=48}^{63} C_t^tail]`, where the tail rule is fixed and the expectation covers initial ages and Bernoulli failures. This is the simulator's additive cost in **simulation units**; no terminal value is added. Full-information centralized control is a valid member of the policy class. A decentralized or two-policy decomposition is a method choice, not part of `𝒜`.

At start of each controlled tick `t∈{0,…,47}`:

1. **Rule admission:** for every machine without a request, if `a_m≥L−1` and it is operating/unassigned, create a PM request. CM is already present for any failed machine from the prior interval. Existing queued requests persist. There is no policy cancellation or choice of intervention type.
2. **Observation and choice:** the controller sees current state/config after rule admission, never the realized future event tape. `A_t` is any subset of `(m,j)` such that `R_m` is queued, `b_j=0`, `d_mj>0`, and no `m` or `j` repeats. `∅` is WAIT. A sequential decoder may build `A_t` with provisional edges/STOP, but only the completed matching is a physical action; no physical time advances during decoding.
3. **Start and interval:** for each pair, mark request started and assign worker for `d_mj` intervals. PM or CM start cost is charged once. Let `S_t` include newly started and already running machines. Charge `c_U` for each machine failed or in `S_t` during this interval, including a job finishing at its end. A healthy machine in neither set increments age by one, capped at `a_max`; if new age `≥L`, an independent Bernoulli(`p`) event can make it failed and charge `c_F`. A failed, unserved machine stays failed and increments physical pending wait. A queued PM not started continues to operate, age and possibly fail.
4. **Completion and lifecycle:** decrement each busy worker's `b_j`. At zero, restore machine age to `floor(a_m(1−r_mj))`, clear failed/pending wait and release worker; close the request. After the interval, a newly failed machine with no request gets a CM request at `t+1`; an unstarted PM request becomes CM at `t+1` and retains its identity/creation time. Costs are `C_t=c_PM·#PMstarts+c_CM·#CMstarts+c_U·#(failed∨servicing)+c_F·#newFailures`; reward is `−C_t`.

The controlled horizon is 48 such intervals. From ticks 48–63 the fixed tail uses the same threshold PM admission and `risk_matching` (not the learned controller); the primary physical outcome is `Σ_{t=0}^{63} C_t = H_cost + tail_cost`. There is no additional terminal salvage/penalty after tick 63: outstanding condition/requests are observed in metrics, but only costs accrued through the tail enter the objective. Hence endpoint truncation and the 16-tick tail can affect ranking, especially for long service or deferred failures; any broader lifetime claim would need a separate horizon study. A controller's known-model forecast is allowed only from present state/config with newly sampled scenarios; actual future tape input is forbidden.

**Observation parity caveat.** The inherited global vector after admission includes age/failure, queue state/age, busy remaining/assignment, compatibility and duration, but not every cost/restoration/failure parameter as a feature. Machine/worker actor inputs include additional configuration fields. A fair `full-information` control must receive the same *available* state/config and admissible action set as the tested policy, with a documented feature manifest. Merely calling two arms “full information” or comparing parameter counts does not prove parity. Configuration values may be constants for a case but still must be disclosed to all model-based controls. Actor/critic asymmetry must be recorded separately.

### Semantics examples (logic checks, **not** prevalence/economic evidence)

| Situation | Correct choice space / implication |
| --- | --- |
| Two queued PM machines both need the sole free compatible worker | Start at most one or WAIT. Serving one now can change the other's failure risk and wait duration. This is immediate and multi-period coupling. |
| A generalist can serve A or B; a specialist can serve only A | Pairing generalist with A may block B although `(A,generalist)` is individually fast. The complete matching and future occupation matter. |
| One admitted PM machine at `L−1`, worker free, no other requests | Start now pays PM/unavailability; WAIT keeps output this tick with risk of failure. Both are legal; the lower expected total cost depends on duration, restoration, p and horizon. |
| `J≥N`, every worker can serve every machine with identical duration/restoration, and no busy job | No capacity bottleneck: choices can be approximately per-machine. F0 and an expanded early-start formulation also agree whenever that formulation chooses no pre-threshold start. |
| Healthy age `<L−1`, worker free | F0 cannot start PM even if model-based economics favor it; the alternative FT below can. This is an action-set/contract difference, not evidence of a policy defect. |
| PM request queued, then machine fails while waiting | At `t+1` the same request upgrades to CM; the machine was productive until failure and never reserved a worker. Cancellation or a service deadline cannot be inferred. |

## Formulation alternatives and literature boundary

| Scenario and formulation | Authority, information, objective/constraints, coupling | Comparison and falsifier |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **F0 (chosen):** centralized maintenance desk dispatches admitted work orders at each tick. | Threshold/automatic CM creates queue; desk chooses legal complete matching/WAIT using present machine/worker state/config; minimize expected H+tail cost with skill, one-to-one and non-preemption constraints. | Existing code and independent qualification can test whether binding alternatives occur and have value; strong full-info matching/rollout controls are feasible. Claim ends at post-admission timing+assignment. |
| **FT/1 (inactive versioned decision contract):** desk is authorized to initiate PM before the threshold when it can start service immediately. | At each H tick, form ordinary threshold queue, then add any healthy, operating, unassigned no-request machine as a *direct-start candidate*. For every selected extra `(m,j)`, create PM request and start it atomically that tick; unselected extra machines create no request. Same observation, physical failure/restoration/cost, non-preemption, CM lifecycle, H+tail and WAIT. Only action eligibility changes. | Based on joint condition-based selection/resource scheduling in [Demirci et al.](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10696-024-09544-y), [Ruiz Rodríguez et al.](https://orbilu.uni.lu/handle/10993/54345) and [Wang & Gombolay](https://cdn.aaai.org/ojs/19839/19839-40-23852-1-2-20220613.pdf), but our exact age/failure parameters remain unvalidated. V2 already tested a bounded early-PM variant and missed its six-tick gate; FT/1 needs an independent operational reason and separate qualification, not a v2 retry. Falsifier: extra actions have negligible exposure or confirmed headroom against *same-action* full-info rollout. |
| **FS (not proposed for implementation):** an external provider accepts work orders with promised windows. | Submitter provides a real deadline/service window; dispatcher assigns qualified workers across periods, with a verified contractual consequence of missed service. Adds a distinct obligation to the objective/constraints, beyond worker occupancy. | [Zamorano & Stolletz](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0377221716305173) establish this mechanism in a provider setting. We lack evidence of such promises and calibrated terms here; do not invent penalties or call queue age a deadline. Falsifier before code: no actual commitment in the intended operation. |

**Implementation and verification burden.** F0 reuses the current enumerated matching set and source-level request invariants; verification is replay of threshold admission, legal matchings and H+tail accounting. FT/1 grows the root action set by adding direct-start healthy machines and needs projection tests proving every F0 action gives the identical transition and event tape, plus tests for atomic request creation and no unchosen request. FS would add a deadline state, a source of promised windows and validated violation treatment; without those, its feasibility and objective cannot be audited. All three admit centralized full-information control. F0 is the only current operative contract; the earlier v2 adapter explored a bounded FT-like action expansion, but its failed gate remains closed. No numerical outcome is inferred from code reuse.

Prior work already studies machine agents that delay/start maintenance and assign heterogeneous technicians ([Ruiz Rodríguez et al., 2022](https://orbilu.uni.lu/handle/10993/54345)), heterogeneous graph scheduling of predictive maintenance ([Wang & Gombolay, 2022](https://cdn.aaai.org/ojs/19839/19839-40-23852-1-2-20220613.pdf)), limited-repairman condition-based timing ([Demirci et al., 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10696-024-09544-y)), and joint selection/routing with skills ([joint operation selection, 2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0305054824001394)). These support the *mechanisms*, not a novelty claim or our numerical simulator assumptions. Distinct empirical contribution would require a specified contract, same-information/action controls and attributable, replicated economic gains; graph, PPO and sequential matching alone are prior art.

| Mechanism and primary evidence | What the study supports | Unconfirmed here / minimum information for real operation |
| --- | --- | --- |

Need actual start/finish/availability records for external calibration. |
| Promised windows and skills: [Zamorano & Stolletz](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0377221716305173) | Real external-provider tasks can have skill constraints and multi-day windows. | No evidence of a promise/SLA in this simulator. Need order creation, promised windows and actual consequences before adding a commitment contract. |
